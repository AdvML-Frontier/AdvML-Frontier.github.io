Human-CenteredTrustworthy Machine Learning

ML vulnerabilities can threaten people’s safety and well-being, yet few venues focus on these real-world harms or the protections people need. Human-Centered Trustworthy Machine Learning brings together ML security researchers, human-subjects experts, and anyone interested in real-world ML security and privacy. We encourage work that grounds technical defenses in practical needs, improves methods for evaluating the real-world impact of ML systems, and strengthens collaboration across these communities.

Call for Papers

We invite human-centered work on trustworthy machine learning systems, especially interdisciplinary research spanning machine learning, security, safety, HCI, ethics, and the social sciences.

OpenReview account required—register early; new accounts can take up to 14 days to activate.

Topics of interest

  1. Impact of Insecurity/Security ML on People: how trustworthy or untrustworthy ML can benefit, harm, or otherwise affect people.
  2. Needs and Acceptability of Trustworthy ML: what forms of trustworthy ML are needed in particular sociotechnical circumstances and how needs vary across people, contexts, and experience levels.
  3. Empirical Methods for Measuring Effectiveness In Practice: human-subjects experiments, internet measurement, surveys, interviews, ethnography, case studies, observational studies, and related social-science methods.
  4. Usable SaTML Systems: studies that evaluate or improve the interfaces and designs of systems relevant to secure and trustworthy machine learning.
  5. Human-Centric SaTML-Relevant Processes: evaluations of human-in-the-loop processes including red teaming, safety practices, and human considerations around ML development and use.
Call for papers

Submission categories

  • Track 1: Regular papers. Research papers of up to 6 pages, excluding references and supplementary material. These papers should present new work, evidence, or ideas related to secure and trustworthy machine learning. Research papers must be novel, well-argued, and ​on​ ​one of the​ ​topics listed​ ​above (see the "Topics of Interest"). Works do not need to be complete and may present results that are in progress and/or unfinished.
  • Track 2: Blue Sky, Position, or Demo papers. Up to 4 pages on visionary ideas, long-term challenges, current debates, overlooked questions, or demonstrations. Completed experimental results are not required.

Submission information

  • Submission template: Submissions must be a PDF file in two-column IEEE proceedings style.

    • That is, authors must use \documentclass[conference]{IEEEtran} when preparing their paper, with the default 10pt font size and page geometry.
    • Using a different template, or modifying font size, margins, or spacing to fit more content, is grounds for desk rejection. The number of allowed pages for a submission depends on the submission category.

  • Supplementary material: There is no limit on references and appendices. However, reviewers are not required to read appendices, and papers are assessed on the body text. Do not rely on an appendix to carry a claim that is central to the paper.

  • Prior and concurrent submissions: In-progress and previously archived submissions are welcome. Concurrent submissions are allowed, but it is the responsibility of the authors to verify compliance with other venues' policies.

  • Anonymity: Submissions do not need to be anonymized.
  • Accepted papers are non-archival: Papers will not appear in formal conference proceedings.
  • Presentation: Each accepted paper will receive either an oral or poster presentation.
  • Attendance for accepted papers: ⚠️ In-person presentations only: We are planning for all presentations at SaTML 2027 to take place in person at the conference. In this edition, we are not planning any remote or virtual presentation option.

  • Usage of LLMs: Following SaTML's policy, if any LLM was used, an "LLM usage considerations" section is required, placed after the Open Science section. Follow SaTML's dedicated “Usage of LLMs” within their CFP for what it must contain.

  • Open Science: Following SaTML's policy, all submissions must include an "Open Science" section immediately before the references, which does not count towards the page limit. In it, the authors should describe which artifacts they are releasing, or explain why sharing is not possible. The section is required for every submission category: Position papers, for instance, often have nothing to release, but the section is still compulsory and should say so. We require all authors to share their artifacts within 3 days after the submission deadline, using a fully-anonymized repository (for example, https://anonymous.4open.science/). After this deadline, the artifact(s) needs to be accessible throughout the review process, and should not be edited. Any violation of the Open Science policy may result in desk rejection. Authors of accepted papers will be required to share their artifacts on https://zenodo.org (see “Important Dates”), and acceptance is conditional on their availability (unless a valid reason is provided). Chairs and reviewers may check that the artifacts are consistent with the claims made in the paper.

  • Ethical Considerations: Following SaTML's policy, each paper may optionally include an "Ethical Considerations" section, placed immediately before the references. This section does not count towards the page limit. In this section, the authors may discuss if they believe the work poses any ethical risk and the steps that are taken to mitigate such risk, also referring to ethics frameworks such as those offered by the Menlo report. If the authors believe that their work does not pose any ethical considerations, this section is not necessary.

Any questions about submissions should be directed to satml-advml-2027@googlegroups.com.

Keynote Speakers

Katharina Krombholz

Tenured Faculty Member · CISPA

Dr. Katharina Krombholz is tenured Faculty at CISPA and head of the Usable Security Group, Empirical and Behavioral Security Research Area. Before that, she was senior researcher at SBA Research. She completed her PhD in November 2016 with distinction. She also received a master’s degree in Media Informatics from TU Wien in 2012.

Ram Shankar Siva Kumar

Data Cowboy · Microsoft

Ram Shankar Siva Kumar is a Data Cowboy working on the intersection of machine learning and security. At Microsoft, he founded the AI Red Team, bringing together an interdisciplinary group of researchers and engineers to proactively attack AI systems and defend from attacks.

His recent book on attacking AI systems, NOT WITH A BUG has been called “Essential Reading” by Microsoft’s Chief Technology Officer and received wide praise from industry leaders at DeepMind, OpenAI as well as policy makers and academia. He is donating his proceeds of the book royalty to Black In AI.

His work on AI and Security has appeared in industry conferences like RSA, BlackHat, Defcon, BlueHat, DerbyCon, MIRCon, Infiltrate, academic workshops at NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, IEEE S&P, ACM - CCS. His work has been covered by Bloomberg, VentureBeat, Wired, and Geekwire. He founded the Adversarial ML Threat Matrix, an ATT&CK style framework enumerating threats to machine learning. His work on adversarial machine learning appeared notably in the National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence (NSCAI) Final report presented to the United States Congress and the President.

He is currently Tech Policy Fellow at UC Berkeley and an affiliate at the Berkman Klein Center for Internet and Society at Harvard University, where he is broadly investigating two questions: How do we assess the safety of ML systems? What are the policy and legal ramifications of AI, in the context of security? He is also Technical Advisory Board Member at the University of Washington.
