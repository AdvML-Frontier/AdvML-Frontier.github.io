Human-CenteredTrustworthy Machine Learning

AdvML Frontiers aims to form an interdisciplinary community of human-centered researchers within trustworthy AI. Although individual researchers rely on human-centered methods and several published SaTML papers include human-factors components, there is not yet a dedicated community around this work. The workshop welcomes senior researchers and PhD students already working on—or interested in—human factors in trustworthy machine learning, with a low barrier to entry for those who want to learn, participate, and network.

Call for Papers

We invite human-centered work on trustworthy machine learning systems, especially interdisciplinary research spanning machine learning, security, safety, HCI, ethics, and the social sciences.

Track 1

Regular papers

Research papers of up to 6 pages, excluding references and supplementary material.

Track 2

Blue Sky, Position, or Demo papers

Up to 4 pages on visionary ideas, long-term challenges, current debates, overlooked questions, or demonstrations.

Non-archival and open to work in progress. Submissions do not need to be anonymized. Concurrent and previously archived work is welcome, and accepted papers will receive either a contributed spotlight or poster presentation.

The submission site will be announced in the near future.

Topics of interest

  1. Impact of Insecurity/Security ML on People: how trustworthy or untrustworthy ML can benefit, harm, or otherwise affect people.
  2. Needs and Acceptability of Trustworthy ML: what forms of trustworthy ML are needed in particular sociotechnical circumstances and how needs vary across people, contexts, and experience levels.
  3. Empirical Methods for Measuring Effectiveness In Practice: human-subjects experiments, internet measurement, surveys, interviews, ethnography, case studies, observational studies, and related social-science methods.
  4. Usable SaTML Systems: studies that evaluate or improve the interfaces and designs of systems relevant to secure and trustworthy machine learning.
  5. Human-Centric SaTML-Relevant Processes: evaluations of human-in-the-loop processes including red teaming, safety practices, and human considerations around ML development and use.

Submission requirements and workshop policy

Format

Use the IEEE template specified by SaTML's call for papers. References and supplementary material do not count toward the 6- or 4-page limits. The submission site will be announced in the near future.

Review and publication

Submissions do not need to be anonymized. Accepted papers are non-archival and will not appear in formal conference proceedings. In-progress, concurrent, and archived submissions are allowed; authors are responsible for compliance with other venues' policies.

Presentation

Each accepted paper will receive either a contributed spotlight talk or a poster presentation. The program deliberately reserves substantial time for an extended poster session and interactive discussion.

SaTML policies

All submissions must comply with the AI Guidelines, Open Science, and Proactive Prevention of Harm policies listed in SaTML's call for papers.
